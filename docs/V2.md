# V2 construction planning

V1 and v1.5 are complete. V2 turns the visual editor into a tool for checking how the assembly fits and operates in the room.

## Implemented first slice: mount positions

- Four fixed presets: Parked, Extended, Swivel left and Swivel right. Camera and inspection depth remain independent.
- TV, soundbar, cradle and TV-side mount rails share one rigid transform. Panel, wall plate, blocking and electrical stay fixed.
- Additional extension and swivel angle are editable in Structure and persist with the project. The selected preview position is session state. Earlier saves load with explicit provisional defaults of 18 inches extra extension and 30 degrees each side.
- Pose-aware drawings, dimension endpoints, camera fit, panel-plane clearance and vertical distance above console top.
- Selected-position collision checks use oriented boxes, including individual cradle members, against panel, wall finish, drywall, console and beam; they also report an assembly extending beyond the modeled wall width.

`MountMotion` is pure geometry. `resolveMountPreview(project, parkedAssembly, preset)` returns a transform, posed envelopes, schematic arm segments and findings without mutating the parked model. Renderers and annotations consume that result. `MotionGroup` applies the same transform to all attached drawings. React does not own motion rules.

The mount drawing uses four hinged arms forming a diamond between the fixed and moving plates. Its arm length is inferred for illustration, not a measured model. The user explicitly deferred extension/swivel coupling and joint-level simulation because these are not needed for build planning. Extension and angular limits remain unverified preferences. Collision checks evaluate the four selected positions, not the continuous movement between them. Panel-plane and console-top distances are signed reference-plane clearances, not minimum distances between arbitrary surfaces. The ceiling/beam collision envelopes remain conservative boxes.

## Hands-on checks

2. In Side and Behind TV, check the arm between the fixed wall plate and moving rails. Change camera or inspection depth and return; the chosen mount position stays selected.
3. Choose Swivel right, open Edit mount range, and reduce extension to zero. Clearance should become negative and collision findings should appear. Restore 18 inches; the initial 30-degree pose should clear the panel.
4. Edit swivel angle, gap and part dimensions. Pose-specific width/gap/cradle dimensions must stay attached to the rotated drawing. Parked returns the normal layout dimensions.
5. Save/export and reload/import edited motion limits. The saved design retains the limits; a new session starts at Parked.

## Construction planning

The user clarified that v2 should help plan construction inside and attached to the wall. External TV/console lead lengths, service slack and bend allowances have been removed. They are not part of this tool's scope.

- **Construction details** switches from the assembly preview to four fixed drawings: framing elevation, mount lag section, upper electrical section and panel edge section. Section cuts use the shared resolved assembly, not separate illustrative geometry. The framing elevation retains the vaulted wall outline.
- Editable stud face width, blocking depth, lag row spacing and proposed embedment replace hard-coded construction assumptions. Spacers bear directly on drywall and occupy cutouts in the slatted finish.
- Separate electrical plate and box/rough-opening envelopes. Plates remain centered below the mount with the specified gap; their width/height and the opening width/height/cavity depth are editable. Box bodies extend to the finished surface, and rough openings interrupt the slats and upper panel. The combined envelopes do not resolve separate power/signal compartments or box hardware.
- Independent proposed in-wall power and signal route offsets plus a route depth plane. Routes flag intersections with studs/blocking and depths beyond the cavity or box backs; these findings indicate unresolved passages, not automatically drilled holes.
- Dimension schedules show floor heights, wall offsets and layer thicknesses. Arrow buttons focus matching inputs in the existing drawer. Earlier saves receive explicit provisional defaults, preserving customized inputs. Retired external-cable fields are discarded if present in a project file.

## Named layout alternatives

Save up to 12 named project snapshots in a separate browser-local library. Compare and apply the layout choices from a selected snapshot, or update a snapshot from the current project. Applying a layout preserves current site measurements, stud positions, part dimensions, hardware facts, source notes and verification status. Comparison shows only settings that will actually be applied: layout dimensions, finishes, soundbar orientation and swivel angle. A duplicate name requires an explicit update. Corrupt libraries and failed writes do not erase existing saved data. Single-project JSON export/import remains unchanged and does not include this library.

## Construction export

Export a standalone SVG sheet from Construction details. It contains all four fixed drawings, dimensional schedules in the selected units, current findings, and unresolved measurement / source-assumption notes. All text is XML-escaped. It is a vector study with model dimensions, not a print-scale fabrication drawing. Actual hardware, electrical compartments, framing passages and mount hole locations still need measured inputs.

## Object categories, layout choices and physical facts

The drawer is organized by thing: **Wall & framing**, **Slatted wall finish**, **Flat panel & spacers**, **TV & mount**, **Soundbar & cradle**, **Console**, and **Electrical & in-wall routing**. A category selector fits the narrow drawer; stable subsection headings express the hierarchy. Placement and spacing sections are uniformly named **Layout**.

- **Layout** fields retain exact entry and sliders. Site measurements and selected part/material dimensions use reference values with explicit Edit / Done actions and no sliders. Existing drawing arrows and checklist links count as explicit edit requests and select the input.
- Physical facts independently show **Verified** or **Needs verification**. Published/supplied reference dimensions retain source information but do not imply the selected physical part has been checked. Changing a fact clears its verified state; reopening it or entering the identical exact value does not. Stud edits use Apply / Cancel. Verification persists in project JSON and browser saves.
- The field audit classifies shelf thickness, box/plate/opening dimensions, material dimensions and actual mount-hole row spacing as facts. TV height, cut panel size, desired gaps, proposed blocking layout and route plans remain choices. Classification is canonical on load, preserving saved dimensions and notes.
- **Calculated** sections contain read-only results and links to controlling fields. Drawing annotations also expose layout/fact/derived intent in tooltips. Construction exports separately list unresolved facts and layout choices.
- **Needs verification** collects only unverified physical facts, grouped into Site measurements, Part & material dimensions, and Hardware details. Each entry links to its ordinary category and selects the fact. Verifying a fact updates the count; fields in their normal sections never reorder by status.
- Saved alternatives replay layout choices against the current physical facts. They do not restore an older wall measurement, part dimension or verified status. Explicit project import/load still replaces the full project.

## Mount installation datums, rail settings and blocking

TV mounting holes are fixed physical facts. The vertical rails can be installed at different usable positions, so hook offsets to the TV edges are derived rather than fixed facts.

- Facts: TV top to upper mounting-hole center, TV hole spacing, the reference rail hole's signed distance below its hook, rail extents above/below the hook, and the available rail mounting shifts. The example shifts are unverified and editable as an arbitrary list; no uniform hole pitch is assumed.
- Choice: selected rail mounting shift. Positive shifts raise both rails and hooks relative to the TV. Hook height = TV top − TV top-to-hole + reference hole-below-hook + selected rail shift. TV-edge-to-hook distances and rail visibility follow from that result.
- Assembled-mount facts: signed hook height above wall-plate top, wall plate dimensions/thickness, lag row offsets measured down from plate top, horizontal lag-column spacing and hole-pattern offset. Plate top = hook height − hook-above-plate-top. Selected wall-plate lag coordinates are independent of spacer layout.
- Blocking can follow the lag-row midpoint or use a manual floor height. Its selected height stays a layout choice. The desired margin beyond rows defines a target coverage band and a finding if the chosen board is too short; it is not a structural edge-distance specification. Selecting manual height starts from the currently derived height.
- Boards occupy full selected stud bays, so the cut schedule does not prescribe floating partial-bay pieces. The chosen coverage-zone width selects bays; actual board endpoints follow measured stud faces.
- **TV & rails** is a fixed front view showing the TV silhouette, fixed TV bolt locations, shifting rails and hook contact line. Protruding rail ends and rail/bolt span mismatches produce findings. The narrow rail profile and linkage remain illustrative.
- Previous saved mount-center and symmetric-row placeholders migrate to provisional datums that preserve their wall-plate / lag heights. They do not become measured hook geometry.

## Separate electrical components and framing passages

Upper power, upper low voltage, lower power and lower low voltage each have independent verified/unverified physical dimensions for their box body, plate and required opening. The component selector in the electrical drawer and section view shares the same selection; checklist / annotation links select the correct component automatically.

Each plate pair stays centered on its datum, with an editable edge gap. Upper plate tops retain the chosen gap below the mount wall plate. Lower plate centers use the chosen floor height and horizontal offset. A box can be referenced to the existing drywall or the finished bearing surface; its physical depth does not stretch to meet the plate. A reserved reach between box and plate is shown separately, with connection / extension hardware explicitly unresolved.

Drywall, slats and upper panel openings follow required-opening dimensions, not plate dimensions or body dimensions. Box/cavity, plate coverage, opening/body, framing and spacer conflicts remain visible. Actual segment intersections with studs/blocking generate red unresolved-passage marks and schedule coordinates. They do not generate a bore diameter or imply that drilling is approved.

Legacy combined electrical envelopes migrate into provisional separate component facts. Custom footprints are split rather than claimed as measured hardware; verify the actual parts before using opening sizes.

## Cut and coordinate schedule

**Cut & drilling schedule** lists blocking/spacer/panel dimensions, proposed lag coordinates from wall/floor, corresponding panel and blocking local coordinates, required electrical openings and spacer-clearance rectangles in each affected layer, with electrical runs assumed vertical. Cuts exclude kerf and fitting allowance; stock / lamination choices, fastener selection and hole diameter remain unresolved.

The vector construction sheet includes framing, rail position, lag section, panel edge, the schedule and unresolved facts. CSV export contains the coordinate schedule in selected units.

### Electrical scope: upper plate height and TV coverage

Power and HDMI are assumed to run vertically from the lower wall beside a stud.
Lateral placement, route depth, framing passage planning, and box-body editing
are outside the interactive workflow. Retained serialized fields support older
saved projects; they do not expose route controls.

The Electrical drawer selects above/below the wall mount and an edge gap. It
compares both locations against the parked TV outline, shows plate top/bottom
floor heights, and reports visual coverage. Outer plate sizes remain editable
physical facts. Lower devices align beneath the upper devices. Routes are
schematic vertical lines; no crossing/penetration conclusions are drawn.

Manual verification: change the gap, compare above/below placement in the drawer
and Behind TV view, then change the rail setting. Upper plates should follow the
mount; the reported floor heights and coverage should update. No lateral route
controls, crossing markers/list, or box-body fields should appear in the drawer.
